\section{The Problem: Grainy Samples}

The forward SDE for the passive model is the OU process
\begin{equation}
dx = -kx\,dt + \sqrt{2T_p}\,dW, \qquad t \in [0, T],
\end{equation}
with marginal distribution $p_t(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_t, \sigma_t^2)$ where
\begin{equation}
\mu_t = e^{-kt} x_0, \qquad \sigma_t^2 = \frac{T_p}{k}(1 - e^{-2kt}).
\end{equation}
The score is trained with a minimum time $t_{\min} = 10^{-3}$ (i.e.\ $t$ is sampled uniformly
on $[t_{\min}, T]$), so the model has \emph{never seen} $t = 0$ during training.

Both samplers integrate backwards from $t = T$ toward $t = 0$ and call the score network
at each step.  The last step reaches $t = 0$, which is out of the training distribution,
so the model returns a garbage score that corrupts the final sample.

\paragraph{PF-ODE (Heun integrator).}
The time grid ends at $t_{\mathrm{next}} = 0$.
The Heun corrector evaluates the score at both $t_{\mathrm{curr}}$ and $t_{\mathrm{next}} = 0$,
so the final update is polluted.

\paragraph{Full SDE (Euler--Maruyama).}
The discrete time grid is $t_i = i\cdot\Delta t$ for $i = N-1, \ldots, 0$.
At the last step $i = 0$, $t = 0$: the model is called out of distribution,
the resulting drift corrupts $x_{\Delta t}$, and no noise is added to partially mask it.

\section{The Fix: Stop at $t_{\min}$ and Apply Tweedie}

\paragraph{Step 1 (PF-ODE).} Change the time grid to end at $t_{\min} = 10^{-3}$
instead of $t = 0$, so the last Heun corrector evaluation stays within the training range.

\paragraph{Step 1 (SDE).} Change the Euler loop to stop at $i = 1$ (i.e.\ $t = \Delta t$)
instead of $i = 0$ ($t = 0$), so the model is never called at $t = 0$.

\paragraph{Step 2 (both).} After the loop stops at $x_{t_{\min}}$,
apply one \emph{Tweedie denoising step} to obtain a clean estimate of $x_0$.

\subsection{Tweedie's Formula}

For a Gaussian perturbation kernel $p(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_t, \sigma_t^2)$,
Tweedie's identity gives the posterior mean:
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_0 \;=\; \mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x_t]
\;=\; \frac{x_t + \sigma_t^2\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x_t)}{e^{-kt}}.
\label{eq:tweedie}
\end{equation}
At $t = t_{\min}$:
\begin{equation}
a \;=\; e^{-k\,t_{\min}}, \qquad
\sigma^2 \;=\; \frac{T_p}{k}\bigl(1 - e^{-2k\,t_{\min}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and one forward pass of the score network at $(x_{t_{\min}},\, t_{\min})$ gives
$s_\theta \approx \nabla_x \log p_{t_{\min}}(x_{t_{\min}})$.
The denoised output is then
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_0 = \frac{x_{t_{\min}} + \sigma^2\, s_\theta(x_{t_{\min}},\, t_{\min})}{a}.
\end{equation}
This requires \emph{one extra NFE} (network function evaluation) but completely
removes the residual noise at $t_{\min}$.

\section{Why It Works}

Because the forward process is Gaussian, $\hat{x}_0$ in \eqref{eq:tweedie}
is the \emph{minimum mean-squared-error} (MMSE) estimator of $x_0$ given $x_t$.
Even if the score is only approximately correct, the estimator degrades gracefully:
any error in $s_\theta$ translates linearly into an error in $\hat{x}_0$.

At $t_{\min} = 10^{-3}$ with typical parameters $k=4$, $T_p = 6.4$:
\[
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{6.4}{4}(1 - e^{-0.008})} \approx 0.11.
\]
Without the Tweedie step, $x_{t_{\min}}$ still carries noise of standard deviation
$\approx 0.11$ (roughly 8\% of the image range), which is perceptible as graininess.
The Tweedie step removes this in a single pass.

\section{Connection to CLD-SGM}

This is exactly the \texttt{denoising = true} flag in the CLD-SGM codebase
(Dockhorn et al., ICLR 2022).  Their \texttt{sampling.py} applies the same step
after every ODE and EM sampler:
\begin{verbatim}
  if config.denoising:
      u = denoising_fn(model, u, 1. - eps)   # ODE sampler
      _, u = step_fn(model, u, 1.-eps, eps)  # EM sampler
\end{verbatim}
where the second return value of \texttt{step\_fn} is the conditional mean $\hat{x}_0$
(the Tweedie estimate), not the noisy sample.

\section{Active Model}

For the active model with state $(x_t, \eta_t)$, the analogous Tweedie step
is applied to the $x$ channels only (since $\eta$ is discarded at the end):
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_0 = \frac{x_{t_{\min}} + M_{11}(t_{\min})\,F_x(x_{t_{\min}}, \eta_{t_{\min}},\, t_{\min})}{e^{-k\,t_{\min}}},
\end{equation}
where $M_{11}(t)$ is the $(x,x)$ element of the joint covariance matrix and
$F_x$ is the network's $x$-score output.
This is exact for the marginal $x$-score in the limit $\tau \to 0$,
and approximate for finite $\tau$.
